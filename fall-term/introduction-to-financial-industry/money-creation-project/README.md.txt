# Money Creation in the Modern Economy

**Course:** Introduction to the Financial Industry  
**Program:** Master of Mathematical Finance (MMF), University of Toronto  
**Type:** Team Project  
**Date:** August 2026

## Overview

This project examines how money is created and destroyed in a modern
banking system, based primarily on the Bank of England article
*Money Creation in the Modern Economy* by Michael McLeay, Amar Radia,
and Ryland Thomas.

The project challenges two common misconceptions about banking:
that banks simply lend out existing deposits, and that central-bank
reserves are mechanically multiplied into loans and deposits.

Instead, the analysis shows that commercial bank lending creates new
deposits, while loan repayment destroys them. It also examines the
constraints on this process and the role played by monetary policy and
quantitative easing.

## Key Topics

- Commercial bank money creation
- Broad money vs. base money
- Loan creation and repayment
- Bank balance sheets
- Liquidity, credit risk, capital, and regulation
- Monetary policy and interest rates
- Quantitative easing
- The relationship between reserves, deposits, and lending
