## Course Overview

Pricing Theory provides a rigorous introduction to modern asset pricing
and derivative valuation, combining economic intuition with mathematical
methods.

The course develops pricing theory from discrete-time financial models
to continuous-time stochastic models, with the no-arbitrage principle
serving as the central framework.

Topics include:

- No-arbitrage and the law of one price
- Replication and hedging
- One-period and multi-period financial models
- Binomial trees and random walks
- State prices and complete markets
- Risk-neutral valuation
- Martingales
- Brownian motion and stochastic calculus
- Itô's lemma
- Black–Scholes option pricing
- Greeks and implied volatility
- Bond pricing and the term structure of interest rates
- Interest-rate derivatives
- Credit risk and credit derivatives

## Assignments

### Assignment 1 — Forwards and One-Period Models

The first assignment applies the no-arbitrage framework introduced in
the opening lectures to forwards and simple one-period financial markets.

Topics covered:

- Valuation of an existing forward contract
- Replication arguments
- Construction of arbitrage strategies
- European option replication
- State-price recovery
- The Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing
- Market completeness
- Pricing additional contingent claims using state prices
